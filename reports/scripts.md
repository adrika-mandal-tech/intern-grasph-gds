# Presentation scripts — the three report pages

Detailed spoken delivery scripts for `insights.html`, `explain.html` and `Datascience.html`. Everything below is written as continuous prose paragraphs rather than bullet points, so each paragraph is one unbroken block you can read straight aloud; paragraphs are separated only by blank lines, and each one ends on the inference rather than stopping at the fact. Recommended delivery order is explain, then insights, then Datascience — orient, story, proof — which runs roughly five minutes, eight minutes and twelve minutes, so about twenty-five minutes plus questions. One line worth repeating at any point in the talk: on a flat table this is only a list of orders, but on the graph it is one story, and distance drives freight, delay and the star rating, and every model on these pages exists to act on that before it happens. A second habit worth keeping is that whenever you quote a decimal you immediately follow it with its percentage, because the percentage is the version the audience remembers afterwards.

## Part 1 — Script for `insights.html` (~8 minutes)

Begin by telling the audience what they are looking at before you show them a single chart. These are one hundred and twelve thousand six hundred and fifty order items, and on a spreadsheet every one of them stands completely alone; the moment you connect each item to its seller, its customer, its product and its zip codes, a single cause — how far the customer sits from the seller — cascades outward into freight cost, delivery delay and the star rating. That is the whole deck in one sentence, and the five hero tiles on the opening screen are the evidence that supports it: fifty-four percent of freight is predictable, seventy-eight percent of lateness is predictable, a late delivery costs one and a ninth of a star, seller communities reach thirty-six percent modularity against a three percent chance level, and inequality across sellers sits at a Gini of zero point seven nine.

Start with the schema, because every later number hangs off it. Items attach to five hubs — order, customer, product, seller and category — and the single edge we derive ourselves, between the customer's zip and the seller's zip, is the distance edge, and that edge turns out to be the root cause of everything that follows. Take a moment here to establish trust in the provenance: every figure on this page comes from the source CSVs, which were verified as zero-different from the stored graph, so nothing here is a sample of a sample or a number produced by a vendor tool. That single sentence buys you permission to be confident for the rest of the presentation.

Now take them to the money slide, which is the distance banding chart. Split the items into distance bands and cost, speed and rating all degrade together, and that joint degradation is the signature of one shared cause rather than three unrelated business problems. Under two hundred kilometres, freight averages twelve point six five reais, six point two percent of orders arrive late, and the rating is four point one seven stars; over three thousand kilometres, freight rises to forty-three point nine five reais, the late rate more than doubles to thirteen percent, and the rating falls to three point eight stars. One cause, three prices. Then give the mechanism, because without it the chart is just a picture: seventy-one percent of sellers sit in São Paulo while only forty-two percent of buyers do, which means the marketplace is manufacturing its own long hauls. The mismatch between where supply sits and where demand sits is the engine of the entire story, and everything else in the deck is downstream of it.

The cascade diagram next to it is worth walking with the cursor because it shows the fork. Distance splits into two parallel consequences — freight cost on one side and carrier transit time on the other — and both of those reconverge on the parcel arriving late, and the parcel arriving late is the thing the star rating actually records. Say the interpretation plainly: distance does not merely correlate with these outcomes, it produces them through a carrier network that has to move the parcel further, which is why fixing geography fixes three things at once.

Move to structure, and here you want to separate two maps the audience naturally conflates. Seller revenue has a Gini of zero point seven nine, which is near-maximal inequality, because the bottom half of sellers earn roughly three percent of the money while the top one percent earn a full quarter of it. But raw volume hides a deeper structural fact: ninety-six point three percent of products have no second seller at all, so the product network falls apart into tiny disconnected islands, and there is almost nothing to cluster or predict there. Link the sellers by shared category instead and the picture inverts into one dense small world, and that dense layer is where real seller communities live, at thirty-six percent modularity against a two point nine percent rewiring null. The inference to land is that a volume leaderboard and a structural leaderboard are two genuinely different maps — PageRank and degree correlate at zero point nine eight five and better, yet their top-ten lists still disagree, so a bridge seller can be structurally central without ever appearing near the top by revenue.

Then come the review charts, which is the moment the audience leans in. Customers punish delay in a clean dose-response curve: on-time orders average four point two one stars with eleven percent low reviews, and by eight to fourteen days late the average collapses to one point six eight stars with eighty percent low reviews. A single late delivery costs one and nine tenths of a star, and that is the strongest effect anywhere in the dataset, far larger than anything about price, which correlates with the rating at essentially zero point zero zero three. The commercial inference is that the repair window is the first week, not the extreme outliers, because the damage is heavily front-loaded and concentrated in the days when intervention is still possible.

The category comparison is the sharpest single finding on the page, so slow down for it. Office furniture is a triple penalty — it simultaneously carries the highest freight at forty point five reais, the worst rating at three point four nine stars, and the most concentrated supply, with only thirty-four sellers and an HHI of zero point four two. Health and beauty sits at the exact opposite corner, cheap to ship, best rated, with hundreds of competing sellers. The point to draw out is that a category's structure predicts its customer experience far better than the products inside it do, which means you can find the bad experiences from the supply-side shape of a category without reading a single review.

Next come the two predictive sections, and the discipline here is to be explicit about what works and what does not. Freight is roughly half-predictable, with R squared at zero point five four meaning fifty-four percent of freight variance explained, and the elasticity decomposition shows why: a one percent increase in distance raises freight zero point one nine percent, weight zero point one five five percent, and price only zero point one zero four percent, so freight is a distance tax rather than a value tax. The honest verdict to give the audience is that a pre-checkout freight quote is feasible at around fifty-four percent, but an exact per-item forecast is not, because the remaining variance is carrier zone pricing that simply is not in the schema. Lateness, by contrast, is roughly three-quarters predictable at AUC zero point seven seven six against a base rate of six point four percent, which means that about ten days before the promised date the model already ranks seventy-seven point six percent of late orders above the on-time ones — enough runway for proactive messaging and early carrier escalation. Then be equally explicit about the failures, because they are the credibility of the page: product co-purchase is a coin flip at zero point five zero, and review score tops out at R squared of eight percent because the rating is set by the delivery clock and not by anything reachable in the graph.

Finish the part by walking the ten findings panel, since it compresses everything into quotable lines. Ninety-one point nine percent of all reviews are four or five stars, so the satisfaction ceiling means any rating model must target the thin one-to-three star tail rather than chase overall accuracy. Weight and distance are near-orthogonal at r minus zero point zero zero six yet both raise freight, so a quote engine needs both terms to handle heavy-local versus light-far parcels. The São Paulo paradox is that São Paulo has the worst seller-side late rate at eight point three three percent among the hubs while a regional seller in Rio Grande do Sul is cleanest at four point four percent, because supply concentration is what spreads long-haul lateness in the first place. Repeat buyers are almost indistinguishable from one-time buyers on first-order rating, at four point one three versus four point zero eight, so churn modelling has to live outside the transaction table. The payment ladder runs credit at one hundred and forty-three point nine reais, boleto at one hundred and twenty-one point nine, debit at one hundred and twenty point seven, and voucher at one hundred and sixteen point nine, which gives a ready-made segmentation feature. And the anomalies are themselves a deliverable: two hundred and thirty-five rating and text mismatches, nineteen dirty seller cities, twenty-four thousand stripped zip codes, six hundred and twenty-three category-less products and one hundred and thirty-one high-late sellers together form five concrete operations queues.

Close the part by telling them that any of this can be re-run directly on GrasPh with the listed queries, that none of the proposed next steps is claimed as already done, and that each one targets a specifically measured ceiling — product links above zero point five zero, reviews above eight percent, freight above fifty-four percent. The single highest-leverage move the data supports is regional fulfilment, because it is the only lever in this entire dataset that touches cost, speed and satisfaction at the same time.

## Part 2 — Script for `explain.html` (~5 minutes)

Open this part as the orientation slide, and set its purpose immediately: before the findings, here is how the analysis was built, so that you can decide for yourself whether to trust the numbers. It runs as ten phases on a read-only GrasPh graph holding five hundred and sixty-eight thousand nine hundred and sixty nodes and seven hundred and seventy-four thousand two hundred and sixty-six relationships, and fourteen of fourteen mathematical proofs pass by executable check rather than by assertion. Tell the audience you will keep this to the shape of the pipeline and the reason each stage exists, and that the detail is on the page if they want it afterwards.

Walk the master pipeline diagram left to right, because the three-stage shape is the whole argument. Stage A proves the data: profile it, which is forty-one checks with zero failures, and fit the heavy tail, which gives alpha two point three eight one and beats both the lognormal and the exponential, so the concentration you will see later is structural rather than an artefact of sampling. Stage B models it: because the raw schema contains zero triangles, nine named projections are the enabling step, and without those projections there is literally nothing to cluster or rank; this is the stage that produces AUC zero point seven seven six and the freight R squared of fifty-four percent. Stage C defends and packages it, with the fourteen proofs, then platform verification, then synthesis into the reports being presented. Then say the discipline out loud, because it is what makes the pipeline trustworthy: no stage re-derives a number from a later stage, and if a pull disagrees with the graph, the assumption is that the pull is wrong and not the algorithm. Point out that the honest negatives survive the whole funnel — review R squared around eight percent, and product links at a coin flip — and that this is deliberate, because a pipeline built to report failure is the only kind worth trusting.

Spend real time on the temporal analysis section, because it is the subtlest thing in the deck and the one a sharp reviewer will probe. The order span runs from September two thousand and sixteen to October two thousand and eighteen, but the boundary months are near-empty: September twenty-sixteen holds four orders, December twenty-sixteen holds one, September twenty-eighteen holds sixteen, and October twenty-eighteen holds four. Modelling those months would let a four-order month masquerade as real seasonality, so they are dropped and every seasonal or trend conclusion runs only inside the clean window of January twenty-seventeen to August twenty-eighteen, which is twenty full months. The inference to make explicit is that this is a correctness gate rather than a convenience: excluding those months is what stops a rounding error from being reported to the business as a trend. Inside that window the numbers are stable, and you should quote them: freight is fifty-four percent explained, with distance at zero point one nine dominating weight at zero point one five five and price at zero point one zero four; delivery days track distance at r zero point three nine three while price barely moves them at zero point zero six two; and shipping inside a single state is late five point eight percent of the time against eight point eight percent across state lines. The only seasonality worth mentioning is mild and visible only inside the clean window, with late rates creeping up in November and December and dipping in February around Carnival. Add the honest caution that twenty usable months cannot validate an annual seasonal model, so the right baseline is a seasonal-naive forecast rather than an over-fitted ARIMA or Prophet model tuned to a single November peak.

Cover the task list quickly, because it is a credibility ledger rather than a narrative: discovery, extraction, profiling, projections, graph algorithms, link prediction, ML, NLP, time series, platform verification, then synthesis. Pull out only the rows where the honesty of the method is visible. Projections are the enabling step, since without them there are no triangles to measure at all. The ML task deliberately contains a negative control so graph value cannot be overclaimed. The NLP task validates that text agrees with stars, with a sentiment-to-score correlation of zero point five five, and its two hundred and thirty-five mismatches become a review-integrity queue that protects the rating analysis from poisoned labels. And platform verification is what proves the shipped data actually matches the stored graph. Every one of those rows removes exactly one failure mode for the stage after it, which is what the funnel diagram is showing.

Finish with reproducibility, which matters to the technical part of the room: the local GrasPh server with raised row limits is the stable backend, the hosted console is for spot-checks only because its authentication is intermittent, and any pull should be cross-checked against a server count before it is trusted. Close by saying that this is the scaffolding — sound data, explicit projections, models with honest negatives, verified provenance — and that the next two pages are the story and the proof.

## Part 3 — Script for `Datascience.html` (~12 minutes)

Introduce this part as the rigorous deep-dive, the one built to convince a sceptical audience rather than to entertain a friendly one. Five research questions are answered in order, each carrying a verdict, each tied to a named graph projection, and each tested against a null rather than asserted. The four tiles give the headline before the detail: Gini zero point seven nine, an eighty percent low-review cliff, one hundred and thirty-one flagged sellers, and a top-category HHI of one point zero zero.

Begin with the framing section, because it sets the standard for everything after. The questions are whether revenue is dominated by a few sellers, whether late delivery explains low ratings, whether real seller communities exist beyond categories, which sellers and categories are fragile, and — the honesty check — whether graph features beat tabular features for rating prediction. Say plainly that the fifth question is a deliberate negative control, and that reporting the ceiling instead of inflating a model is precisely what gives every other number in the report its value. Also note the graph discipline on this slide: per the field manual no stored relationship carries a date, an amount or a count, so every edge weight in the report is derived from node properties, and every projection is explicitly named rather than implied.

Move to concentration, and use the Lorenz curve to make inequality intuitive rather than abstract. The curve plots sellers from poorest to richest on the horizontal axis against cumulative revenue on the vertical, the dashed diagonal is perfect equality, and the shaded gap between them is the inequality, which reads as a Gini of zero point seven nine. Translate it immediately: the bottom half of three thousand and ninety-five sellers generate about three percent of revenue, the top one percent generate twenty-five point seven percent, and half of all item volume comes from just one hundred and thirty-one sellers. The concentration bars make the same point in a form an executive can quote — the top five percent take forty-nine point one percent, which means the next four percent after the very top add only about twenty-three percentage points rather than the naive multiple. The power-law fit with alpha two point three eight one beats the alternatives, so this is structural rather than noise, and the inference is that the marketplace is operationally dependent on a small group, which makes seller loss a first-order business risk: losing roughly nine sellers would erase a quarter of revenue.

Then the strongest effect in the dataset, which is the delay cliff. Mean review falls monotonically while the share of one and two star reviews climbs: on-time orders sit at four point two one stars and eleven percent low reviews, one to three days late at three point two stars, four to seven days late at two point zero nine stars, and eight to fourteen days late at one point six eight stars with eighty percent low. Frame it as a relation between two quantities so the causality is legible — the review score falls two point five three stars across the delay buckets while the low-review share swings up sixty-eight point seven percentage points — and then give the insight: a ten-day delay converts a normal four-star order into a one-star order, which is why the first week of a delay is the only intervention window that pays for itself. Be precise at the same time that this is a correlation of minus zero point two five eight across one hundred and eleven thousand reviewed items, and that it is confirmed rather than proven on this page, because delay still drives the classifier later in the deck.

Explain why delay clusters geographically, and use this as the hand-off into the map. São Paulo moves the most items by far at eighty thousand five hundred and sixty-four, which is fifty-three point four percent of all items, and it also carries the worst late rate at eight point three percent, while a regional seller in Rio Grande do Sul is cleanest at four point four percent. São Paulo sellers serve the most long-haul orders, and long haul is what makes parcels late, so the supply concentration that makes São Paulo the commercial capital is the same concentration that spreads its lateness nationwide. That paradox is the geographic root of the entire causal chain.

Now the communities, where the nuance is what matters. Leiden modularity is zero point three five seven against a rewiring null of zero point zero two nine, which is twelve times stronger than chance, and the partition is stable across ten seeds with modularity only moving between zero point three five five and zero point three five nine and NMI between zero point seven and zero point eight three. Two further checks matter for honesty: Leiden agrees with the deterministic multilevel method at zero point three four nine, and the category-only projection is far weaker at zero point one zero zero, which proves seller communities are not simply category groups. The inference to draw out is that structural centrality and commercial rank are related but not identical, because PageRank and degree correlate at zero point nine eight eight while the top-ten lists still differ on every projection. So a bridge seller whose products are widely shared can be structurally central without ever being a volume leader, which means targeting by volume alone misses some of the sellers who actually hold the network together.

Turn to fragility, which answers where the marketplace would break. Per-category HHI reaches one point zero zero where a single seller owns the category outright and zero point eight nine where two sellers do, while office furniture, with thirty-four sellers, sits at zero point four two. The marketplace-wide picture is diversified, so the inference is that the risk hides in a thin long tail that any single global score would hide, which is exactly why the recommendation is a queue to investigate rather than a blanket action. The at-risk seller queue applies the same logic one level down: of eight hundred and eighty-six sellers with at least twenty reviews, one hundred and thirty-one are flagged for a late rate of fifteen percent or more or a mean score at or below three and a half stars, and the worst run thirty to forty-nine percent late against an eight point three percent hub benchmark. Be scrupulous here and say that this is a reason to investigate and not proof of fraud, because this dataset cannot establish intent.

Walk the master causal chain box by box, since it is the spine of the whole presentation. Distance is the root cause, and it raises freight with an elasticity of zero point one nine, giving R squared of fifty-four percent explained. It also lengthens carrier transit at r zero point five six nine, which is the dominant leg and far larger than seller handling at zero point one nine or approval at zero point zero four. That makes the parcel late, and that is what the star rating records. Because every link is individually significant and monotone, this is a chain and not a coincidence. The commercial punchline is that freight is a distance tax rather than a value tax, and therefore regional fulfilment is the only intervention in the entire dataset that attacks cost, speed and satisfaction simultaneously.

Next come the two diagnostic matrices, and they are best read as the technical proof sheet behind everything already said. On the left, the correlation matrix shows what actually drives what: carrier transit is the largest delivery-time driver at zero point five six nine, distance is second at zero point three nine three, and price is essentially noise at zero point zero six two, while delay hits reviews at minus zero point two five eight and price barely moves them at zero point zero zero three. On the right, the confusion matrix shows what the lateness model really does at a six point four percent prevalence: AUC zero point seven seven six, which is seventy-seven point six percent concordance, average precision zero point two four seven, accuracy eighty-five point three percent, F one of zero point six three five, and recall on the late class of sixty-five point seven percent. In practical terms the model catches roughly seven thousand true lateness cases, and the false alarms are the cheap half of the trade rather than a reason to discard it.

Then the map, which is the interactive centrepiece of the page. Every Brazilian federal unit is shaded, and the buttons on the left switch the lens between late rate, order items, mean price, freight proxy and revenue, with the readout beside it updating the top states, the national median and the commercial implication. On late rate the North stands out, with Amapá at eleven point two percent and Roraima at ten point one percent, alongside the São Paulo hub at eight point three percent, while the South is cleanest at four point four percent. On order items, São Paulo alone is fifty-three point four percent of the total with Minas Gerais a distant second at around six percent, so demand is a South-East concentration and the North is a rounding error. On revenue, São Paulo holds eight point six million reais, which is the dominant hub by an order of magnitude. On the freight proxy, Acre is the worst at two point eight times the São Paulo intra-city baseline, so the far interior pays two to three times as much to move the same parcel. The insight to leave them with is that the revenue core, the lateness peak and the freight bill are the same geography, and that overlap is precisely the argument for regional fulfilment rather than a general efficiency drive.

Move to machine learning, and keep the leakage point prominent because it is what separates a real model from a notebook trick. The first model predicts lateness at purchase time using only features known before shipping — never the delivery date, which would be answering itself — and both models split on time, training before mid-year twenty-eighteen and testing after. It reaches AUC zero point seven seven six against a logistic baseline of zero point six five nine, giving roughly ten days of deployable early warning. The second model predicts ratings, and adding seller-graph features moves mean absolute error only from zero point nine seven four to zero point nine seven one, with R squared at eight point one percent against a mean score of four point zero three out of five. The inference is that the rating is set by the delivery clock and nothing in the graph beats it, so eight percent is the honest number rather than a failure of the model.

Cover the hypotheses compactly, because the section is a verdict ledger. Concentration is supported, the delay-to-rating link is supported, communities are real but modest, structural centrality equals commercial top only partially, graph features beating tabular is not supported, and single-seller dependency is supported. The value of the section is that it reports the wins and the ceilings together, which is what lets the audience trust the wins.

Close on the mathematical foundations and then the summary. Fourteen executable proofs back every number in the report, so anything said in the other two decks traces back to one of them, which is how you can claim that PageRank genuinely converges rather than half-converges, that modularity is bounded and therefore a reading of thirty-six percent is real, that Adamic-Adar cannot be faked by a hub, and that the bootstrap p-values are conservative so nothing overclaims significance. Then give the three takeaways: revenue is dangerously concentrated, delay is the dominant driver of dissatisfaction, and regional fulfilment is the one lever that improves cost, speed and satisfaction together — while everything else is a queue to investigate rather than a proven cause.

## Appendix — answers to keep in your pocket

If someone asks whether this is real data, say five hundred and sixty-eight thousand nine hundred and sixty nodes and seven hundred and seventy-four thousand two hundred and sixty-six relationships, verified as zero-different from the source CSVs and read-only. If they ask how accurate lateness prediction is, the answer is AUC zero point seven seven six, which is seventy-seven point six percent concordance, against a six point four percent prevalence, and measured at purchase time rather than after the fact. If they ask whether the communities are real, yes, with modularity zero point three five seven against a null of zero point zero two nine and stability across seeds. If they ask why review prediction is weak, it is a ceiling effect, because the mean is four point zero three out of five, so an R squared of eight percent is the honest number rather than a failure of the model. If they ask whether anything beats the delivery clock, no, because graph features add roughly three tenths of a mean-absolute-error point and nothing more. If they ask about cross-sell, be straight that it is barely better than a coin flip, since only three point three percent of orders carry two or more distinct products. If they ask which states matter most, point at São Paulo, where fifty-three point four percent of items, eight point six million reais of revenue and the eight point three percent late rate all sit in the same place.

Two delivery notes before you start. Turn on the method and caveat notes when the audience is technical and you want the guardrails visible, and leave them off for an executive room. Rehearse exactly two beats, the distance cascade in insights and the master chain in Datascience, because those two carry the entire talk, and if you land them then everything else is supporting evidence.
